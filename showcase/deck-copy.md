# Glider deck copy

Ten slides. Headline at most 5 words, supporting line at most 12 words, speaker note 1 to 2 sentences.

## Pick one

**Title options**
1. Glider
2. Glider: reading, without the fight
3. Glider, a reading lens

**Tagline options**
1. Reading that keeps up with you.
2. Hard pages, made easier to read.
3. It notices when reading gets hard.

Recommended pair: title 1 with tagline 3. It says the one thing no other reading tool does.

---

## 1. Title

- **Headline:** Glider
- **Line:** It notices when reading gets hard.
- **Note:** Glider is a Chrome extension for students with dyslexia. It reshapes any web page so it is easier to read, and steps in when you get stuck.

## 2. The problem

- **Headline:** The web is dense
- **Line:** About one in ten people are estimated to have dyslexia.
- **Note:** Course pages and articles are built for fast readers: tight lines, long words, no way to slow the page down. For a dyslexic student every assignment starts with a fight against the layout.
- **Check before use:** the one-in-ten figure is the British Dyslexia Association's estimate. If you cannot confirm it in time, use the no-number line instead: "Most pages are built for fast readers."

## 3. What it is

- **Headline:** One lens, any page
- **Line:** A Chrome extension that makes text easier to read.
- **Note:** One switch. It works on the pages students already use, with no account and nothing to set up.

## 4. The lens

- **Headline:** Room to read
- **Line:** Wider spacing, larger text, bold word starts.
- **Note:** Turn the lens on and the page opens up: more space between letters, words and lines, and the start of each word in bold to guide the eye. Turn it off and the page is exactly as it was.

## 5. Tune it from the edge

- **Headline:** Tuned from the edge
- **Line:** Size and spacing, one reach away.
- **Note:** Glider sits at the edge of the screen. Reach for it and set size and spacing to what feels right; it remembers on every page.

## 6. Hear any word

- **Headline:** Hear any word
- **Line:** Long words split into chunks you can hear.
- **Note:** Hover a long word and it breaks into chunks, each with the sound it makes written underneath. Tap a chunk to hear it, or have the whole word sounded out.

## 7. Look-alike words

- **Headline:** Their, there, they're
- **Line:** Easily confused words come with a quick meaning.
- **Note:** Words that are easy to mix up get a quiet underline. Hover one and each word in the group shows a few-word meaning, so you know which one you are reading.

## 8. It notices you are stuck

- **Headline:** It sees you stuck
- **Line:** Your webcam notices when a few lines slow you down.
- **Note:** With eye tracking on, Glider learns your normal pace from a short calibration. When your eyes linger on the same lines well past that pace, it knows exactly where the trouble is.

## 9. The finale: it makes it simpler

- **Headline:** Then makes it simpler
- **Line:** Those lines, rewritten in plainer words, right in place.
- **Note:** The paragraph you are stuck on grows and is replaced by a simpler version: common words, shorter sentences, same meaning. One tap brings the original back.

## 10. Close

- **Headline:** Read at your pace
- **Line:** Runs on your device. No account.
- **Note:** The lens, the word help and the eye tracking all run in the browser; no video ever leaves the camera frame. Glider is free to try today.

## Notes for whoever assembles the deck

- **Slide 10 accuracy.** "Runs on your device" is true for the lens, word lens, look-alike words and eye tracking, and for the built-in simplifier. The optional better rewrite by Claude sends the one stuck paragraph to a server. If the finale scene shows the Claude rewrite, keep the line as written but do not add "nothing leaves your device".
- **Not a diagnosis.** Nothing in the copy says Glider detects or diagnoses dyslexia. Keep it that way in any added text.
- **Slide 8 wording.** "A few lines", not "a word": a webcam can tell which lines, not which word.
- **Alternate headlines** if any feel flat on the slide: 4 "The page opens up", 5 "Yours in one reach", 6 "Sound it out", 8 "It knows where", 9 "Simpler, in place", 10 "Reading, your way".
- Voice throughout: plain and confident, no exclamation marks, no emoji.
