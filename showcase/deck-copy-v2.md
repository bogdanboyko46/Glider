# Glider deck copy, version 2

Written to a dyslexic reader, in second person. Ten beats, ten different layouts. No slide numbers or kickers on the slides themselves; the numbers below are only for assembling.

Rules kept throughout: headline at most 5 words, line at most 12 words, no statistics, no hype words, no emoji, no exclamation marks, and nothing that says Glider diagnoses anything.

---

## 1. Title

- **Headline:** Glider
- **Line:** Built for the way you read.
- **Layout:** Wordmark huge and centred, line beneath it, logo strokes entering from the right edge; nothing else.
- **Art note:** Soft morning light across cream paper with a faint pale-sage wash, one thin dark-green thread drifting in from the right edge.

## 2. The problem

- **Headline:** Pages crowd you
- **Line:** Tight lines. Long words. No room to slow down.
- **Layout:** Headline small in the top-left corner; a cramped page fills the rest and bleeds off the right and bottom edges.
- **Art note:** Dense overlapping layers of pale-sage tracing paper, tightly stacked and slightly out of register, darker green shadow where they pile up.

## 3. What it is

- **Headline:** A lens for dyslexia
- **Line:** It reshapes any web page to suit how you read.
- **Layout:** Split 50/50: text block on the left half, the screen on the right half, a clean vertical seam between them.
- **Art note:** A single sheet of cream paper with one clear circular patch of brighter, calmer light, like a lens resting on it; sage edges.

## 4. The lens

- **Headline:** Lines stop crowding you
- **Line:** More space, larger text, and a bold start to every word.
- **Layout:** Screen full width across the top two thirds; headline and line sit in a caption bar underneath.
- **Art note:** Evenly spaced horizontal bands of soft light on cream, like sun through wide blinds, with generous pale-sage gaps between them.

## 5. Tuned from the edge

- **Headline:** Set it your way
- **Line:** Press the logo at the side. Tune size and spacing.
- **Layout:** Screen pushed to the left and cropped; headline right-aligned down the right edge, beside the logo strokes.
- **Art note:** The right edge of a cream page catching light, five short dark-green stitches running in from that edge, the rest calm sage.

## 6. Hear any word

- **Headline:** Hear the hard word
- **Line:** Long words split into chunks you can hear.
- **Layout:** One enormous word broken into chunks fills the centre; headline small above it, line small below.
- **Art note:** Gentle concentric ripples in pale sage spreading from one point on cream paper, like sound moving through still water.

## 7. Look-alike words

- **Headline:** Their, there, they're
- **Line:** Words that look alike come with a quick meaning.
- **Layout:** Three cards side by side across the middle, one word each; headline centred above, line centred below.
- **Art note:** Three near-identical pressed leaves laid in a row on cream paper, each in a slightly different green.

## 8. It notices you are stuck

- **Headline:** It sees you stuck
- **Line:** Your camera notices the lines that slow your eyes.
- **Layout:** Screen large on the right with a soft highlight band across a few lines; headline stacked vertically at far left, one word per line.
- **Art note:** A dark-green thread crossing cream paper in smooth lines, then looping back on itself in one small knot before continuing.

## 9. Makes it simpler

- **Headline:** Then it gets simpler
- **Line:** Those lines, in plainer words, right where you are.
- **Layout:** Before and after stacked: the hard paragraph small and faded at top, the simpler one large below; headline between them.
- **Art note:** A tangled dark-green thread on the left that straightens into one clean, calm line toward the right, on warm cream.

## 10. Close

- **Headline:** Read at your pace
- **Line:** Runs on your device. No account.
- **Layout:** Headline centred low on the slide, line beneath it, small logo and "Glider" at the bottom centre; wide empty space above.
- **Art note:** A small paper glider crossing an open, pale-sage sky above a cream horizon, long soft shadow, lots of empty air.

---

## Notes for assembling

- **Who it speaks to.** Every line addresses a dyslexic reader as "you". No "students", no "everyone", no "users".
- **Slide 5 accuracy.** There is no power button or switch on the edge bar. The reader presses the logo at the side of the screen to open the panel and turns the lens on there; the copy says "press the logo".
- **Slide 8 accuracy.** "The lines", not "the word": the camera can tell which lines, not which word.
- **Slide 10 accuracy.** "Runs on your device" holds for the lens, word help, look-alike words and eye tracking. The better rewrite sends the one stuck paragraph to a server, so do not add "nothing leaves your device".
- **Slide 3 wording.** "A lens for dyslexia" names who it is for; it does not say Glider detects or diagnoses anything.
- **Alternates** if a headline does not fit its layout: 2 "Too much, too tight", 3 "Made for dyslexic readers", 4 "Room to read", 5 "Yours in one press", 6 "Sound it out", 8 "It knows where", 9 "Simpler, in place", 10 "Reading, your way".
- **Art notes** are all abstract and text-free, in pale sage, cream and dark green, so type and screens can sit on top.
