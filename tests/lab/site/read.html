How honeybees find their way

Honeybees live together in large groups. A single hive can hold tens of thousands of bees, and each one has a job to do. Some clean, some guard the door, and some fly out to look for flowers.

When a bee finds a good patch of flowers, she flies home and tells the others. She does this with a dance on the wall of the hive. The other bees crowd around her and follow her moves.

Prior to commencing their foraging flights, young worker bees frequently perform orientation flights in the vicinity of the hive in order to memorize the location of the entrance; subsequently, they utilize this information to navigate home after travelling approximately three kilometres.

The dance is called the waggle dance. The bee runs in a straight line while shaking her body from side to side, then loops back and does it again, many times over.

The orientation of the dance demonstrates the direction of the food source in relation to the sun, whereas the duration of the waggle portion indicates the distance; numerous observers have attempted to ascertain how accurately recruits interpret these signals, with considerable variation between colonies.

In winter there are no flowers, so the bees live on the honey they stored in summer. They pack together in a tight ball in the middle of the hive to stay warm.

A colony that has accumulated a sufficient quantity of honey prior to the onset of winter is able to maintain a temperature of approximately thirty-four degrees at the centre of the cluster, as the workers generate heat by rapidly contracting their flight muscles without moving their wings.

Beekeepers frequently examine their hives in order to detect indications of disease at an initial stage, since numerous infections are able to spread rapidly through a colony; additionally, they endeavour to facilitate the bees’ access to water during prolonged periods of heat.

In spring the hive grows again. The queen lays more eggs, the young bees take over the work, and the first flights of the year begin on the warm days.
